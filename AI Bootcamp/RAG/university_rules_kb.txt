The University Academic Policy dictates that students must maintain a minimum attendance of eighty percent in all registered lectures, tutorials, and laboratory sessions throughout the semester. Failure to meet this requirement without an officially sanctioned medical excuse will result in an automatic grade of F in the respective course and disqualification from taking the final examination.

The Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) is calculated on a four-point scale at the end of each academic semester. Grades ranging from A to F are assigned numerical points from four down to zero, weighted by the semester credit hours of each course. A CGPA below two point zero places a student on academic probation for the subsequent semester.

Academic dishonesty, including plagiarism, cheating during examinations, impersonation, and unauthorized collaboration on graded assignments, is subject to strict disciplinary action. First-time offenses result in a zero mark for the assessment and a written reprimand, while severe or repeated violations may lead to suspension or permanent expulsion by the University Disciplinary Committee.

Undergraduate students are permitted to drop a registered course within the first four weeks of the semester without any penalty on their academic transcript. Course withdrawals requested between the fifth and tenth weeks will be recorded with a grade of W, whereas withdrawals attempted after the tenth week will automatically be assigned an F grade.

The University Financial Aid and Scholarship Program provides merit-based and need-based financial assistance to eligible full-time students. Merit scholarships require maintaining a minimum CGPA of three point five five, while need-based grants require demonstrating verified household income constraints through official documentation submitted before the annual deadline.

Students wishing to transfer credit hours from another accredited institution must submit official transcripts and detailed course syllabi to the Academic Standards Committee. Credits are eligible for transfer only if the earned grade is a B or higher and the course content aligns at least eighty percent with the university curriculum.

The campus Information Technology and Network Usage Policy mandates that all students use their assigned university credentials responsibly. Unauthorized access to administrative systems, bandwidth abuse, peer-to-peer file sharing of copyrighted material, and running unauthorized network servers on campus Wi-Fi are strictly prohibited and monitored by IT Security.

University Dormitory Regulations require all resident students to adhere to night curfew hours established at ten post meridiem on weekdays and eleven post meridiem on weekends. Overnight visitors are not permitted without prior written approval from the Hall Warden, and possession of hazardous materials or alcohol inside resident halls results in immediate eviction.

The University Central Library allows undergraduate students to borrow up to five books simultaneously for a maximum duration of fourteen days. Overdue items incur a daily fine, and failure to return borrowed books prior to semester examinations will result in the withholding of official transcripts and examination hall tickets.

During final examination periods, students must present their valid University Identification Card to enter the examination hall. Electronic devices, including mobile phones, smartwatches, programmable calculators, and digital notes, are strictly forbidden inside the exam hall and will be confiscated immediately upon discovery.

Science and engineering laboratory safety guidelines require all students to wear certified safety goggles, lab coats, and closed-toe footwear prior to handling chemical substances or operating electrical equipment. Eating, drinking, or operating personal electronic devices inside research and teaching laboratories is prohibited at all times.

Students seeking a formal leave of absence or semester deferral must apply through the Registrar Office prior to the third week of the semester. Deferred semesters count toward the maximum allowable degree completion duration of six years for four-year undergraduate programs unless an official medical deferral is granted.

The University Student Health Center provides free primary medical care, first aid, and basic mental health counseling services to registered students during weekday operational hours. In cases of severe medical emergencies occurring outside operational hours, campus security arranges immediate transport to the nearest affiliated teaching hospital.

Graduate research students pursuing Master of Science or Doctor of Philosophy degrees must defend their research proposals before a thesis advisory committee by the end of their second semester. Research dissertations undergo mandatory similarity indexing via anti-plagiarism software and must achieve a similarity score below fifteen percent before final defense scheduling.

Course enrollment registration takes place online during pre-assigned registration windows based on student credit completion status. Senior students receive priority access to course enrollment, followed by juniors, sophomores, and freshmen. Over-subscription permits require explicit approval from the Department Chair.

The University Code of Student Conduct expects all members of the campus community to promote a safe, respectful, and inclusive environment. Physical harassment, verbal abuse, property destruction, and discrimination based on gender, ethnicity, or creed are subject to immediate suspension pending an investigation by the Student Grievance Panel.

Continuous assessment in undergraduate courses comprises mid-semester examinations, laboratory reports, quizzes, class participation, and semester projects. The total weight of continuous assessment must range between forty and sixty percent of the overall course grade, with the remaining weight assigned to the final comprehensive examination.

International students holding university-sponsored student visas must maintain full-time enrollment of at least twelve credit hours per semester to comply with national immigration regulations. Any reduction in course load below full-time status requires prior authorization from the International Student Services Office.

The Office of Career Development and Alumni Relations offers career counseling, resume building workshops, mock interviews, and annual campus recruitment fairs. Undergraduate students must complete a mandatory eight-week industry internship after their sixth semester to fulfill degree graduation requirements.

University sports facilities, including the indoor gymnasium, swimming pool, tennis courts, and football ground, are open to students presenting valid ID cards during designated operational hours. Intramural sports tournaments take place each spring semester under the supervision of the Athletics Department.

Tuition fee payment deadlines are strictly enforced at the start of each academic term. A late fee penalty is assessed for payments made past the due date, and accounts overdue by more than thirty days result in temporary suspension of online portal access and course registration privileges.

The University Student Government consists of elected student representatives who advocate for student interests, organize cultural events, and manage recognized student clubs and societies. Elections take place annually during the fall semester under the supervision of an independent Election Board.

Disability and Accessibility Services provide reasonable academic accommodations, including extended exam duration, assistive technology, and accessible classroom seating, to students with documented physical or learning disabilities upon registering with the Support Office.

Campus security guards operate twenty-four-hour patrols and manage access control points at all university gates. Parking on campus premises requires a valid university vehicle decal, and unauthorized parking in disabled or faculty spots results in vehicle towing and administrative fines.

The University Research Grant Scheme allocates competitive funding to undergraduate and graduate students proposing innovative research projects under faculty supervision. Proposals are evaluated biannually on scientific merit, feasibility, and potential societal impact.

Student counseling services offer confidential individual therapy sessions, stress management workshops, and crisis intervention support. Appointments can be scheduled online or in person at the Student Development Center with qualified mental health professionals.

Graduation audit procedures require graduating seniors to submit a formal application for degree clearance at the beginning of their final semester. Clearances must be obtained from the library, finance department, residential halls, and academic department prior to diploma issuance.

Emergency evacuation procedures are posted in all university buildings, classrooms, and residential halls. Mandatory fire drills take place once per semester, and students are required to follow designated assembly point instructions during emergency alarms.
